# Uniform average mixing on paths of order 4p−1

Research note, 27 November 2026. No author name assigned. AI-assisted draft; not peer-reviewed or formally verified in Lean. A targeted search found no earlier proof of this family; priority is not guaranteed.

## Theorem
For an odd prime p, P_(4p−1) admits uniform average mixing under a probability distribution of readout times if and only if p≥5. For p≥5 one may choose a finite distribution supported on nonnegative integers, with at most 4p²−2p+1 distinct times. No explicit universal bound on the largest time is asserted.

## 1. Spectral target
Put q=4p, N=q−1, and θ_r=2 cos(rπ/q), r=1,...,N. The eigenprojectors E_r are given entrywise by (2/q) sin(krπ/q) sin(lrπ/q). As in Kang (2026, forced coherences lemma), it suffices that the averaged cosines of every distinct spectral pair equal −1/N when r,s have the same parity and 0 otherwise. Indeed if D=δ_kl and F=δ_(k+l,q), finite trigonometric sums give sum_r E_r(k,l)^2=(2+D+F)/(2q). The odd and even projector sums are (I+H)/2 and (I−H)/2 with H the reversal matrix, so the sum of their entrywise squares is (D+F)/2. Substituting the target in the spectral expansion yields (q sum E_r(k,l)^2−(D+F)/2)/(q−1)=1/N.

For p=3, Kang's nonexistence theorem for q=12 applies. We prove the positive cases below.

## 2. Exactly one positive-energy relation
Set m=2p−1 and ζ=exp(iπ/(4p)), a primitive 8p-th root. Its minimal polynomial is Φ_(8p)(X)=(X^(4p)+1)/(X^4+1)=sum_{k=0}^{p−1} (−1)^k X^(4k), of degree 4p−4. Suppose sum_{r=1}^m a_r θ_r=0 over Q. Then T(X)=sum a_r(X^(m+r)+X^(m−r)) vanishes at ζ, so Φ divides T. The quotient has degree ≤2. Both T and Φ are reciprocal with respect to degrees 2m and 2m−2, respectively (allow leading coefficients zero); hence the quotient has the form u(X²+1)+vX. The coefficient of X^m in T is zero. Since m≡1 (mod 4), that coefficient is ±v, and v=0. The remaining relation comes from (X²+1)Φ: its coefficients at m+r are ±1 for odd r and 0 for even r. Thus the relation lattice is generated by a primitive vector ε supported precisely on the p odd positive-energy indices. For p=5 the odd signs are (+,−,−,+,+).

By Kronecker's density theorem the closure of the real-time phase orbit in the m-dimensional torus is H={φ: sum ε_r φ_r=0 mod 2π}. The integer-time orbit has the same closure: an integer character k is trivial on that orbit iff k·θ∈2πZ. All θ_r are algebraic while π is transcendental, so this happens iff k·θ=0. The character criterion for torus subgroups gives the claim. Extend phases by φ_(2p)=0 and φ_(4p−r)=−φ_r.

## 3. Auxiliary phase distribution
Write a=1/(4p−2). We construct a law on H for which every same-parity distinct spectral-pair cosine has mean −a and every opposite-parity cosine has mean 0. Use independent laws on the odd and even positive-energy coordinates. Write z=exp(iφ), with C=Re z and S=Im z. All phases are fourth roots of unity. The needed moments are E C=0 (odd group) or −a (even group), E S=0, E(2C²−1)=−a, and for distinct coordinates of the same group E(C_i C_j)=−a and E(S_i S_j)=0. Mixed sine/cosine moments vanish by global conjugation symmetry (they are not needed for cosine differences). Work first with the signed odd phases ε_r φ_r, whose fourth roots must have product 1; reverse those signs afterwards.

### 3.1 Odd group for p≥7
Define r_o=1/2−pa/[2(p−3)] and d_o=ap(p−1)/(p−3). Both are nonnegative and d_o≤r_o≤1, since r_o−d_o=(p−6)/[4(p−3)] and 1−r_o≥1/2. Make (p−3)/2 independent pairs of fourth roots. In each pair:
- with probability d_o, choose (1,−1) or (−1,1) equiprobably;
- with probability r_o−d_o, choose two independent fair real signs;
- with probability 1−r_o, choose two independent fair imaginary signs ±i.

For each coordinate E C=E S=0, E C²=r_o. Within a pair E C_1 C_2=−d_o and E S_1 S_2=0. To enforce product 1, take three reserve roots: choose the first two independently and uniformly from all four roots; set the third to the inverse of the product of every root chosen so far. Conditional on all pairs, each reserve root is uniform and any two reserve roots are independent uniform. Consequently every cross-pair or reserve-pair moment vanishes, even though the total product is fixed. Uniformly permute all p coordinates. Then

E C=E S=0,
E C²=((p−3)r_o+3/2)/p=(1−a)/2,
E C_i C_j=−d_o (p−3)/[p(p−1)]=−a,
E S_i S_j=0.

The law is invariant under simultaneous conjugation; inversion of individual coordinates according to ε leaves the necessary cosine and sine-pair moments unchanged. Product 1 before sign reversal ensures support on H afterwards.

### 3.2 Odd group for p=5
Use the previously published P19 draft's five patterns in units of π/2, with probabilities:
(0,0,0,0,0): 1/96;
(0,0,0,2,2): 25/96;
(0,0,2,3,3): 5/36;
(0,1,1,3,3): 5/72;
(1,2,3,3,3): 25/48.
Uniformly permute and conjugate with probability 1/2. Each exponent sum is 0 mod 4. Direct rational averaging gives the same moments with a=1/18 (see checker). Reverse the prescribed signs as above.

### 3.3 Even group for every p≥5
Let l=p−1 (even), r_e=(1−a)/2, d_e=(l−1)a+(l−2)a². Make l/2 independent pairs of cosine values, using this law:

| pair | probability per ordered pair |
|---|---:|
| (1,−1), (−1,1) | d_e/2 |
| (1,0), (0,1) | (r_e−a−d_e)/2 |
| (−1,0), (0,−1) | (r_e+a−d_e)/2 |
| (0,0) | 1−2r_e+d_e |

All probabilities are nonnegative: d_e>0, r_e−a−d_e=(4p²−5p+4)/[4(2p−1)²]>0, r_e+a−d_e is larger, and 1−2r_e+d_e=a+d_e>0. They sum to 1. Replace cosine 1 by root 1, cosine −1 by root −1, and each cosine 0 by an independently chosen fair imaginary sign. Single-coordinate moments are E C=−a, E C²=r_e, E S=0. Within a pair E C_i C_j=−d_e, while across pairs E C_i C_j=a². All distinct sine-pair moments vanish. After a uniform permutation, the probability two distinct coordinates share a pair is 1/(l−1); hence E C_i C_j=[−d_e+(l−2)a²]/(l−1)=−a. The other moments remain unchanged. No product constraint is imposed on this even group.

### 3.4 Full spectral pairs
For reflected copies of different positive energies, cos(φ_i±φ_j)=C_i C_j∓S_i S_j, so the moments above handle either sign. For a positive mode and its own reflected copy, cos(2φ_i)=2C_i²−1. For pairs with the zero mode, use E C=−a for even and 0 for odd. Opposite-parity pairs of nonzero modes have zero mean by independence and the zero cosine and sine means of the odd law. Thus all distinct spectral pairs have exactly the auxiliary target. Call this finite phase law ν.

## 4. Exact real and integer time existence
Let F:H→R^(N choose 2) record the cosine of each distinct spectral-phase difference. Let K=conv F(H), compact by finite-dimensional Carathéodory. Normalized Haar measure h on H has mean F equal to zero, because each difference character is nontrivial (its restriction to the real-time flow has nonzero frequency). Haar measure has full support, so 0 belongs to ri K: if a supporting functional relative to aff K attains its maximum at the mean, continuity and full support force it to be constant on F(H), a contradiction to a proper supporting hyperplane.

Let ρ=(4p−2)ν/(4p−1)+h/(4p−1). Its F-mean is exactly the target y of section 1. As a strict convex mixture with a relative-interior point, y belongs to ri K. The set D={F(tθ):t∈Z} is dense in F(H) by section 2. Therefore closure(conv D)=K and ri(conv D)=ri K (standard finite-dimensional convexity). In particular y belongs to conv D, not only to its closure. All times can be made nonnegative because F(−tθ)=F(tθ).

## 5. Support bound
For m=2p−1 positive modes, positive differences of full spectral energies are drawn from 2θ_i, θ_i, θ_i+θ_j and θ_i−θ_j for i<j. Their coefficient vectors are distinct and the difference of any two has support size at most four. The sole relation from section 2 has support p≥5, so no two forms coincide. Thus the number is 2m+2 binom(m,2)=4p²−2p. All F coordinates for equal frequencies coincide, so aff K has dimension at most this number. Carathéodory yields at most 4p²−2p+1 integer times; discard zero weights and merge repeated times. Section 1 gives the required exact uniform matrix. QED (candidate proof; external audit pending).

## Scope and verification
The new random pairing construction is analytic; the finite checker uses exact fractions and tests all 24 primes between 5 and 101. These tests are supplementary, not a substitute for the general proof. The p=5 law and spectral-target/relative-interior method reuse the earlier P19 manuscript and Kang's framework. The new contribution is the general constrained phase distribution and resulting family theorem, with the integer-time strengthening. No numerical bound on the maximum time is supplied for general p.
