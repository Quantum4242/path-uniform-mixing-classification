# Random-time uniform mixing on paths with prime-power odd part

Research working note, 27 November 2026. No author assigned. AI-assisted, not independently refereed, not Lean-formalized. Do not claim priority before a fresh literature audit. This is a proof draft with supplementary exact checks, not a publication package.

## Candidate classification
Put q=N+1 >= 3. Suppose q has at most one distinct odd prime divisor (q is a power of two, or q=2^k p^ell with p odd prime, k>=0, ell>=1). Then the adjacency walk on P_N admits uniform average mixing under a probability distribution of times precisely unless q is a multiple of 6 with q>=12. In every positive case there is a finite probability law on nonnegative integer times. In the new cases with p>=5, the support bound is m(m+1)+1 for even q, m^2+1 for odd q, where m=floor((q-1)/2). For odd powers of 3 the bound m^2+1 also holds as an upper bound, without claiming all gap forms distinct.

We use the spectral coherence criterion of Kang, arXiv:2609.14463v1 (2026): it suffices that all distinct same-parity spectral pairs have mean cosine -1/N and opposite-parity pairs mean 0. The standard path eigensystem and mirror identity prove sufficiency; see that paper, equation (4). Kang proves the negative cases 6|q, q>=12 and positive cases q prime, twice prime, power of two, including a continuous density. The new range treated below consists of odd prime powers, and their even multiples by powers of two when p>=5. The q=9 case had been identified in prior AI-assisted work and is not claimed as new; verify priority separately for all cases. The q=4p argument from the preceding research note is used as a base case for even q with h=2; that note is included.

## 1. The arithmetic is a disjoint parity-check code
Write theta_r=2 cos(r pi/q), 1<=r<=m, for the positive eigenvalues. Set zeta=exp(i pi/q). Relations among them correspond exactly to reciprocal polynomials T(X)=sum_r c_r (X^(m+r)+X^(m-r)) divisible by Phi_(2q), with zero coefficient at X^m. All assertions about rational relations below follow from irreducibility of the cyclotomic polynomial.

**Even q=2hp**, h=2^(k-1)p^(ell-1). Here m=hp-1 and Phi_(2q)=sum_{j=0}^{p-1} (-1)^j X^(2hj). The quotient T/Phi is reciprocal of degree at most 2h-2 (reciprocal with respect to that full degree even if leading coefficients vanish). Its middle coefficient at h-1 is zero: m=h-1 modulo 2h and the middle coefficient of T vanishes. A basis consists of X^s+X^(2h-2-s), 0<=s<h-1. In positive-index coordinates the corresponding relation is supported on r=+d or -d modulo 2h, d=h-1-s. These blocks are disjoint, each with p entries and coefficients +/-1. Free coordinates are multiples of h, p-1 in number. Each block is entirely of one parity. If h is even, each parity has h/2 blocks counting the free group of size p-1 as one even block. If h is odd, each parity has (h-1)/2 constrained blocks and (p-1)/2 free coordinates. This follows directly by counting residue classes in 1..hp-1.

**Odd q=hp**, h=p^(ell-1). Here m=(hp-1)/2 and Phi_(2q)=sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(-1)^j X^(hj). The quotient is reciprocal of degree at most h-1 with middle coefficient zero, because m=(h-1)/2 modulo h. Basis X^s+X^(h-1-s) for 0<=s<(h-1)/2. Positive-index blocks r=+d or -d modulo h, d=(h-1)/2-s, have size p and disjoint support. Free coordinates are multiples of h, (p-1)/2 in number. Since h is odd and the two exponents s,h-1-s have the same parity, the coefficient at m+r times (-1)^r is constant within each block. Thus after the sign change psi_r=(-1)^r phi_r the relation on each block is sum psi_r=0 modulo 2pi.

For either case the relation basis is also an integral lattice basis: disjoint supports with unit coefficients imply every integral relation has integral coefficients in the basis. Thus the phase orbit closure is exactly the subtorus H defined by the independent block-product-one constraints, after the stated sign changes (for even q use the +/-1 relation coefficient as sign for each coordinate). On free coordinates in the even case use no sign change.

## 2. From phase laws to genuine integer times
Every theta_r is algebraic. If k is an integer vector and k dot theta=2 pi l with integer l, transcendence of pi implies l=0. By the character criterion for closed subgroups of a torus, the closure of integer times is the same H as for real times. This works also for any fixed nonzero algebraic sampling step.

Let F:H -> R^(N choose 2) record all pairwise cosine differences after inserting reflected phases phi_(q-r)=-phi_r and, when q is even, phi_(q/2)=0. All these characters are nontrivial on H (their time frequencies are nonzero), so Haar probability on H has F-mean zero. It has full support; a proper supporting hyperplane of K=conv F(H) cannot contain its mean, since continuity would force the supporting functional constant on all of F(H). Hence 0 is in ri K. If an auxiliary phase law realizes a positive scalar multiple (strictly greater than one) of the required coherence vector, mixing it with Haar gives the target in ri K. Density and ri(conv D)=ri(cl conv D) for D=F(Z theta) imply the target belongs to conv D, not merely its closure. Finite convex combination, nonnegative times by cosine symmetry. Caratheodory: for even q the possible positive frequency forms are theta_i, 2theta_i, theta_i +/- theta_j (with positive difference), hence at most m(m+1); for odd q there is no zero eigenvalue, at most m^2. Add one for support. For p>=5 the disjoint relation blocks have minimum nonzero support p, so different formal gap forms cannot coincide (their difference has support <=4), although distinctness is not necessary for the upper bound.

For the already known positive cases use Kang's existence of a density directly. If its mean were on the relative boundary, a supporting real analytic cosine polynomial would be constant almost everywhere with respect to that density, hence constant on a positive Lebesgue measure set and identically constant. This contradicts a proper supporting hyperplane. The same density/relative-interior argument applies to any algebraic sampling step.

## 3. Odd q: a root-of-unity cluster construction
For q=hp odd, after the sign change of section 1, it is sufficient to arrange E Re(z_i conjugate(z_j))=-b for all distinct positive coordinates, and E Re(z_i z_j)=0 for all i,j including i=j, where z_i=exp(i psi_i). This gives same-parity spectral cosine -b and opposite-parity zero, including reflected modes. Indeed same-parity positive pairs use differences after sign change, opposite-parity pairs use sums, and reflection flips both parity and phase. We aim for b strictly larger than b0=1/(hp-1).

Use p-th roots of unity and global rotation by a uniform p-th root; this preserves each constrained product and kills all first moments and all E z_i z_j, since p is odd. A constrained block is either active, in which all its p roots equal one root, or inactive, in which the p distinct roots are uniformly permuted. Both products equal one. Treat each free coordinate as an additional node: if inactive use an independent uniform root, if active use its assigned root. Total nodes G=(h+p-2)/2. All nodes have activation probability r=(1-(p-1)b)/p. Within a constrained block the mean cosine is [p r-1]/(p-1)=-b. Between nodes, arrange shared activation in clusters; inactive phases are independent across nodes with mean zero.

For p>=5, h>=p, choose uniform perfect matching on G nodes, adding one dummy if odd. Let K=G-1 if G even, G otherwise. A pair of nodes shares a match with probability 1/K. For each matched pair independently, activate both with probability r, otherwise both inactive. In the active case assign roots with uniform marginals and real cross moment -d=-b K/r. This is achievable whenever d<=cos(pi/p): mix independent roots with two roots separated by (p-1)/2 steps, randomized by a uniform rotation. At b=b0 we have b/r=1/(h-1). If h=p, G=p-1 even and d=(p-2)/(p-1)<cos(pi/p), by cos x>=1-x^2/2 and pi^2<10. If h>=p^2, d<=G/(h-1)<=1/2+1/(2(p+1))<cos(pi/p). Strictness permits b>b0 by continuity. Distinct nodes thus have correlation -r d/K=-b. Free dummy ignored. This proves every odd prime power p^ell with p>=5, ell>=2.

For p=3 and h>=9 (ell>=3), replace matching by a uniformly relabeled partition into triples plus a remainder of size 0,1 or 2. Let s_max=sum over cluster sizes u of u(u-1)/(G(G-1)). Within each active cluster assign distinct cube roots uniformly, giving mean cosine -1/2. Mix that partition with the all-singleton partition to achieve pair co-clustering probability s=2b/r. At b0, s=2/(h-1), whereas s_max>=2(1-2/G)/(G-1)>2/(h-1), since G=(h+1)/2>=5. Thus b>b0 is possible. For h=3 (q=9), there is one constrained block and one free root. In the active block choose its root Z uniformly, and choose free root -Z with probability b/r, otherwise independently uniform on cube roots. Inactive block uses balanced roots, free independent. The free root need not itself be a cube root, only its distribution needs global rotation symmetry. This gives desired cross correlation, and b/r<1 for b near 1/8. This also has positive margin, but q=9 is not asserted novel.

## 4. Even q: pairwise marginals with reserve coordinates
Now q=2hp even, p>=5, h>=4 (h=1 known, h=2 prior note). Let a=1/(q-2). The auxiliary target is -a for same-parity full spectral pairs, zero otherwise. Use independent laws for the two parities of positive eigenvalues; the parity containing zero mode hp has cosine mean -a, the other has zero. Write z=C+iS in fourth roots in sign-adjusted coordinates. Required moments: E C=mu0 (-a or 0 as above), E S=0, E C^2=(1-a)/2; E C_i C_j=-a and E S_i S_j=0 for distinct same-parity coordinates. They give E cos(2phi_i)=-a, either sign of pair phase difference, and zero-mode constraints. Opposite parities have zero correlation by independence and one parity's zero means. Inverting the relation signs preserves these moments. The auxiliary target is strictly larger in magnitude than 1/N, so section 2 applies.

Choose active coordinates uniformly within each block, independently across blocks. A block with three reserves has all reserve marginals uniform and pairwise independent conditional on the active values: choose two independent uniform fourth roots and set the third to the inverse of their product times the active product. In free blocks any unused nodes are simply independent uniform roots. Let alpha_i be active probability, beta_ij joint active probability. Thus alpha=(p-3)/p in a constrained block, and beta=(p-3)(p-4)/(p(p-1)) within a block, alpha_i alpha_j across. Set active means mu_i=mu0/alpha_i and active real probabilities r_i=1/2-a/(2alpha_i); all active sine means zero. Conditional on the active sets, match the active nodes with a specified matching distribution. Different pairs are independent. For any edge, let c_ij be probability of being matched conditional on both active. If a matched pair has E C_i C_j=-D and E S_i S_j=0, then its unconditional cosine product is beta_ij[-c_ij D+(1-c_ij)mu_i mu_j]. Thus choose

D=(a/beta_ij+(1-c_ij)mu_i mu_j)/c_ij.

A valid matched pair law: assign (1,-1),(-1,1) cosine values each probability D/2; assign (1,0),(-1,0) probabilities (r_i+mu_i-D)/2,(r_i-mu_i-D)/2; assign (0,1),(0,-1) analogously for j. Set remaining mass L=1-r_i-r_j+D on (0,0). Replace every zero cosine by fair imaginary signs, independent unless specified otherwise. This is a probability law if 0<=D<=min(r_i-|mu_i|, r_j-|mu_j|); L>=0. If a sine product Y is desired, choose the signs in (0,0) with correlation Y/L, permissible if |Y|<=L. This preserves all single-coordinate moments. A dummy in the matching can just be discarded, drawing the actual node from its own marginal.

### 4.1 p>=11: uniform global matching suffices
Use three reserves per constrained block and all free coordinates active. Put alpha=(p-3)/p, beta_min=alpha(p-4)/(p-1). In a parity group with n<=hp/2 nodes and f<=p-1 free nodes, the number of active nodes M<=alpha hp/2+3. Add one dummy if odd, let K be the number of other nodes in the resulting even set, so K<=M. Uniform matching gives c=1/K. Since a=1/(2hp-2), K a<=alpha/4+(7/2)a. Also beta>=beta_min>=28/55, alpha>=8/11, a<=1/86. Therefore

D <= K a/beta_min + K a^2/alpha^2
<= 5/14+(7/2)(1/86)/(28/55)+(1/4+(7/2)/86)(1/86)/(8/11)^2
=1469415/3313408 < 655/1376 = 1/2-3/(2*86*(8/11)) <= r_i-|mu_i|.

Here alpha/(4 beta_min)=(p-1)/(4(p-4))<=5/14. This proves validity for every edge and both parities.

### 4.2 p=7: a small internal-matching mixture
Use four active and three reserve nodes in each constrained block. If h even, g=h/2>=2, the free group (size 6) uses four active nodes, and M=4g. If h odd, g=(h-1)/2>=3, free group size 3 uses two active nodes, M=4g+2. In both cases alpha_i>=4/7, beta_same>=2/7, beta_cross>=16/49. Put K=M-1. With probability 1/K match internally within each active block; otherwise use a uniform global perfect matching. Cross conditional probability c=(K-1)/K^2; same block conditional probability at least s=c+1/(3K). Since (2/7)s >= (16/49)c, D is bounded by a(1+a)/[(16/49)c]. For h even, g>=4 this bound <= its g=4 value < 0.452, while r-|mu|>=0.476. Indeed a=1/(28g-2), 1/c=4g+1/(4g-2); both a and a/c decrease with g. For h odd g>=3, a=1/(28g+12), 1/c=4g+2+1/(4g), giving bound <= its g=3 value <0.454 while r-|mu|>=0.472. These comparisons use exact fractions in the checker. For h even g=2 (q=56), the unbiased parity has two constrained blocks, where the cross bound a/[(16/49)c]<0.464<r. The biased parity has a constrained block and a free block, so cross beta=(4/7)(2/3) and D<=a(1+a)/(beta c)<0.405. Within blocks D<0.388 for either parity. All are below the biased minimum r-|mu|>0.451. No g=3 even-h case occurs for p=7. This proves p=7.

### 4.3 p=5: two reserves and an exact covariance correction
Three reserves would leave too few active nodes. Instead choose three active nodes and two reserve nodes in each constrained block. For h even let g=h/2: the free group size 4 has three active nodes and one independent uniform reserve, M=3g. For h odd let g=(h-1)/2 (even, since h is a power of 5): the free group size 2 has both active, M=3g+2. Thus alpha=3/5 in constrained blocks, alpha_F=3/4 or 1 in free blocks. For constrained same-block pairs beta=3/10, gamma=1/10 is probability both reserves. Free same-block beta_F=1/2 or 1. Put K=M-1 if M even, M if M odd (dummy). With probability u=1/K use the following special matching: choose one internal pair among each constrained triple, and among free triples (if present); pair the free active pair internally (if present); uniformly match the remaining singletons, including dummy. Otherwise use uniform global matching.

Let base=(K-1)/K^2. Conditional match probability within a constrained block is s=base+1/(3K). Between constrained blocks c=base+1/(9K L0), where L0=g-1 for even g, g for odd g in even-h case, and g-1 for odd h. Between constrained and free blocks cF=c for even h, base for odd h. Free within-block sF=base+1/(3K) or base+1/K. These formulas follow from the 1/3 chance that a specified active triple node remains a singleton in the special mode. All probabilities positive when relevant. For different blocks, and free same-block edges, use D from section 4 and sine product zero.

Complete the two reserves of a constrained block by choosing one uniform root and setting the other to the inverse of it times the active product. Conditional on active values each reserve individually is uniform, but the two reserves need not be independent. Conditional on a particular two-reserve choice, let P be the product of the three active roots. Then E(C_res1 C_res2)=E Re(P)/2 and E(S_res1 S_res2)=-E Re(P)/2. Given the matching, the active triple either has exactly one internal pair (probability 3s) or none. If internal, its third root is independent of the internal pair and has mean mu. If none, all three independent with mean mu. Give a matched constrained internal pair cosine moment X and sine moment Y; its complex product expectation is X-Y (by conjugation symmetry). Thus E Re(P)=3s mu(X-Y)+(1-3s)mu^3. The required unconditional distinct constrained same-block moments are

beta[s X+(1-s)mu^2]+gamma E Re(P)/2=-a,
beta s Y-gamma E Re(P)/2=0.

Solve by T=X+Y=-a/(beta s)-(1-s)mu^2/s and

Y=[mu T/2+(1-3s)mu^3/(6s)]/(1+mu), D=-X=-T+Y.

Use the pair law of section 4 with this D,Y. It suffices to check D>=0, D<=r-|mu|, 0<=Y<=1-2r+D, and the ordinary D margins for cross and free edges. The companion `verify_p5_polynomials.py` checks these as rational function inequalities for the entire ranges, not just sample g: for each rational expression, shift g=g_min+x and expand numerator and denominator as polynomials; all coefficients are nonnegative and denominator constant is positive. The ranges are even h even g>=2 (biased cross constrained edge only if g>=4), even h odd g>=5, odd h g>=2. Use mu=0 or -a/alpha, mu_F similarly. All probability marginals are strictly feasible except harmless zero sine correction in the unbiased group. This is a finite symbolic certificate of the general inequalities. In the proof note the formulas are explicit so the polynomial checks can be reproduced independently.

Finally, the reserve completion across different blocks is conditionally independent given active phases, with each reserve individually uniform. Hence all cross-block moments computed above are unchanged, and the adjusted within-block moments are exactly the target. This proves p=5 for all even q in the new range.

## 5. Scope and remaining work
This classification covers moduli with at most one distinct odd prime factor. Moduli containing two or more distinct odd prime factors remain outside the proof (except previously known exclusions). Their relation supports can overlap, so the disjoint completion argument is not automatic. The proofs use standard cyclotomic and convexity tools, and a new random matching / conditional completion construction. A strong potential contribution is the unified classification on the stated arithmetic class, not merely additional isolated P_N examples.

The p=5 symbolic checks, finite coefficient and moment checks, and prior q=4p checker are provided. Finite computations alone do not verify the analytic steps. The exact symbolic inequalities cover their entire stated ranges if the expressions have been transcribed correctly. The present note is not externally reviewed and is not a complete Lean proof. Further independent review of the probabilistic construction and priority search are needed before public release. No minimal support or maximum time bound is asserted.
