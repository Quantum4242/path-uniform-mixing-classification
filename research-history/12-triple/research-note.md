# Uniform average mixing on four odd paths with three prime factors

## Exact orbit certificates, quantitative moment interior, and timing-noise correction

Research record, 27 November 2026. No author assigned. AI-assisted; not independently peer-reviewed or fully Lean-formalized. This is a working supplement, not a public release.

### 1. Results and scope

Let q=N+1>=3, A the adjacency matrix of P_N and M(t)=|exp(itA)|^{circ 2}. We ask for a probability measure on nonnegative real times giving mean M=J_N/N, simultaneously for every initial vertex. Kang (arXiv:2609.14463v1) proves the forced cosine moments and classifies parity collisions; his positive theorem covers prime, twice-prime and power-of-two q. His negative theorem excludes 15|q, 21|q and 6|q with q>=12. Our ongoing conjecture is that these are the only obstructions. No complete proof or counterexample to that conjecture is obtained here.

**Theorem 1 (computer-assisted).** Uniform average mixing exists for q=385,429,455,561 (P_384,P_428,P_454,P_560). For each q, there exists an auxiliary phase law with same-parity off-diagonal cosine moments -1/(q-2), and opposite-parity moments zero. In each case the exact arithmetic certificate establishes a strictly positive real solution of a finite linear system. It does not require storing that solution as enormous rational fractions. The verified bounds are reported in verification.log. The auxiliary phase support is NOT a set of physical time samples.

**Theorem 2 (quantitative correction).** Suppose an odd-q path has the stronger phase law in Theorem 1. Then an open l1 ball of radius 1/(2N) about the desired cosine moment vector lies in the relative interior of the full moment convex hull, in coordinates indexed by distinct positive spectral gaps. Consequently there are finite readout laws on every nonresonant time grid, including every grid with positive algebraic step. Furthermore, for any known symmetric independent timing-noise law supported in [-1/(2N),1/(2N)] (in units where A is the dimensionless Hamiltonian), one may choose grid times and probabilities so that the noisy readout still gives exactly J_N/N. The chosen law can depend on the noise law. This is not a guarantee for a fixed protocol under arbitrary unknown noise. This also implies existence of smooth compactly supported physical time densities. The latter existence statement in already known cases is prior work; we make no universal novelty claim for the general Fourier/convex tools.

### 2. Exact phase law from the cyclotomic field

For odd q put m=(q-1)/2 and alpha_j=2 cos(2 pi j/q), 1<=j<=m. The full physical spectrum theta_r=2 cos(r pi/q) is alpha_j at r=2j and -alpha_j at r=q-2j. Let zeta=exp(2 pi i/q), and let V_j be the integer coefficient vector of (X^j+X^(q-j)) modulo the monic cyclotomic polynomial Phi_q, in the power basis of Q(zeta). Let d=deg Phi_q. Every seed s in {0,1,2,3}^d defines a rational-linear real functional on Q(zeta), assigning angle (pi/2) s_k to the kth power-basis element. The phase of alpha_j is therefore i^(s dot V_j mod 4). Every integer relation among the energies vanishes on these angles (even before reducing modulo 2 pi). By Kronecker's character theorem this angle vector is in the continuous-time orbit closure H. Thus a fourth-root phase law is a sufficient construction, not an exhaustive representation of H.

Average each seed over the Galois action by all units u modulo 2q, with the physical permutation r -> min(ur mod 2q, -ur mod 2q). This preserves parity and corresponds to rational-linear automorphisms of the cyclotomic field. For odd q, on alpha indices the action is j -> min(uj mod q,-uj mod q); the signs of the physical modes are retained. Cosines are even under exchanging the two indices and under simultaneous negation of both phases. Therefore it suffices to check orbits of alpha-index pairs: (i<j) for differences and (i<=j) for sums. This reduction is exact and covers ALL original pairs, including an opposite-parity pair with i=j. It is not an approximation to the full problem.

For each orbit O, let B_{O,k} be the sum of the cosines of the relevant difference or sum over distinct pairs in O for seed k. These entries are integers. The Galois-averaged moment is B_{O,k}/|O|. Add a first row of ones. Let the integer vector b have first entry q-2, entries -|O| for difference orbits, and zero for sum orbits. We require B w = b/(q-2), w>0. For the four examples the selected square B has respective orders 416,494,482,624. No row is discarded in these examples. The discovery script checks normalized character signatures modulo four and can discard genuinely identical rows, but the independent verifier reconstructs every orbit without that pruning and asserts the square dimension. In general one must check any discarded row is exactly redundant, not just equal on a finite random sample.

### 3. Verified positive solution without exact rational elimination

Store integers Q=10^12, R0 (square matrix) and u0 (vector). Define R=R0/Q, u=u0/Q, y=b/(q-2). Discovery uses a floating LP and approximate inverse, but verification independently reconstructs B and b from the raw seeds and the full physical eigenmode permutation; it never loads the LP matrix or floating weights. All certificate entries are checked using bounded int64 products with explicit overflow preconditions and Python Fraction for the final bounds. SymPy is used only to generate the exact monic cyclotomic polynomial, independently reduced by backward long division. No trigonometric floating evaluation is needed because phases are fourth roots of unity.

Let epsilon=||I-RB||_infty<1, r=||R||_infty and e=||y-Bu||_infty. If Bx=0 then ||x||<=epsilon||x||, hence B is invertible (it is square). The exact solution w=B^-1 y satisfies w-u=R(y-Bu)+(I-RB)(w-u), giving ||w-u||_infty<=r e/(1-epsilon). If min(u)>r e/(1-epsilon), all exact weights are positive. The first row gives sum w=1. This proves the exact rational linear system has a positive solution, despite not expanding its fractions. The verifier checks all these inequalities with rational arithmetic, and checks that the orbit coverage equals N(N-1)/2. The four checks cover 424265 original unordered spectral pairs in total. Positivity lower bounds (rounded down conservatively): 7.0352e-5, 3.6680e-6, 6.0133e-5, 3.1267e-5 respectively.

### 4. Full moment geometry and exact physical times

Let Lambda be the finite set of distinct positive gaps |theta_r-theta_s|. On H define F_omega as the real part of the associated phase character. Equal gaps give identical functions on H because their characters agree on the dense real-time orbit. If two pairs with the same gap have conflicting required moments, no solution is possible; our auxiliary certificates already establish consistency. The desired vector y indexed by Lambda equals -1/N on same-parity gaps and 0 otherwise. Let v be the stronger auxiliary vector with -1/(N-1) and 0. By Section 2, v belongs to C=conv F(H). The spectral projector identity D=sum E_r^{circ 2}=(2J+I+S)/(2q), with S the reversal matrix, and sum of squares of parity projector sums=(I+S)/2, gives the exact target J/N for these moments; alternatively use Kang's forced-coherence theorem.

**Lemma (explicit l1 ball).** If h is Haar probability on H, distinct positive-gap cosines are orthogonal, have mean zero, and have squared norm 1/2. Indeed the underlying characters are nontrivial; a sum or difference frequency cannot be zero except the difference of identical gaps. For every real vector x with sum |x_omega|<=1/2, the nonnegative function 1+2 sum x_omega F_omega is a probability density with respect to h and has moment vector x. Hence the closed l1 ball of radius 1/2 is contained in C. In particular C is full dimensional in distinct-gap coordinates.

Now y=((N-1)/N)v. By convexity the ball of radius 1/(2N) centered at y is contained in C (mix v with a point in the zero-centered ball in proportions (N-1)/N and 1/N). The *open* ball lies in int C. This is an explicit margin in all distinct-gap coordinates, not just the Galois-invariant subspace. This matters: approximating phase points by physical times can break Galois symmetry, so naively solving only the reduced rows at approximate physical times would not establish uniform mixing.

Let delta>0. The nonresonance condition is: for all c in Z^N, delta sum c_r theta_r in 2 pi Z implies sum c_r theta_r=0. Its failure is restricted to a countable set of steps delta=2 pi k/(sum c_r theta_r)>0 for nonzero integers k and nonzero denominator. Every positive algebraic delta satisfies it by transcendence of pi. Kronecker's theorem and compact recurrence show the nonnegative integer orbit with step delta is dense in H, as is every tail and its translation by t0 theta. Hence C is the closure of the convex hull of the actual sampled vectors. The relative-interior equality for convex sets implies every point in int C is a finite convex combination of actual sampled vectors. In particular y is attainable. Caratheodory gives at most |Lambda|+1 <= m^2+1 time points for odd q. This argument gives no upper bound on the largest sample index.

### 5. Exact timing-noise correction

Let independent timing noise X have a known symmetric distribution supported on [-h,h], h<=1/(2N). Its characteristic function at each gap is real, c_omega=E cos(omega X). Since |omega|<=4 and cos x>=1-x^2/2, c_omega>=1-8h^2>=1-2/N^2>0 (N>=2). We can compensate for noise by selecting a law of planned times with cosine moments y'_omega=y_omega/c_omega. Independence and symmetry imply E cos(omega(T+X))=c_omega E cos(omega T), so these moments restore y exactly.

For odd q the number of distinct same-parity positive gaps is at most m(m-1)/2=N(N-2)/8: the even eigenmodes give one set of alpha differences, and the odd modes are their negatives. Therefore

  ||y'-y||_1 <= [N(N-2)/8] (1/N) [ (2/N^2)/(1-2/N^2) ]
              = (N-2)/(4(N^2-2)) < 1/(2N).

The last inequality follows from N(N-2)<2(N^2-2) for N>=2. Thus y' lies strictly inside C, and Section 4 produces a planned time law on every nonresonant grid. Choose a sufficiently late tail so that T>=h, ensuring T+X>=0. The exact final average is J/N. For a physical Hamiltonian H_phys=J_c A, dimensionless time is |J_c|t_phys/hbar, so this bound scales accordingly; no experimental implementation or noise-agnostic robustness claim is made. Taking the noise to have a symmetric C-infinity bump density with compact support inside [-h,h] yields a finite mixture of translated bumps, i.e. a smooth compactly supported nonnegative time density achieving exact mixing.

### 6. A rigorous proof/disproof framework for future work

For any fixed q, the orbit closure H can be represented by finitely many integer-character equations from a lattice basis of all energy relations, with each phase represented by a pair of real variables on the unit circle. Negative exponents are conjugates on the unit circle. Every F_omega is a polynomial in these pairs. Thus membership of y in C can be expressed as a finite existential real-algebraic sentence using at most |Lambda|+1 phase points and nonnegative convex weights; quantifier elimination is a theoretical decision method for this *closed-convex-hull* question. If y is outside C, strict separation yields a trigonometric polynomial nonnegative on H whose linear evaluation at y is negative, a rigorous impossibility certificate. If y is in the interior, exact finite grid mixing follows. Boundary membership alone does not guarantee actual time mixing, and infeasibility in the fourth-root subclass is not a counterexample. This gives a clear route for finding a genuinely new obstruction, possibly using nonnegative-polynomial certificates, without treating numerical LP failure as a proof.

### 7. Reproduction, limitations and references

Run `python verify_certificate.py 385 429 455 561` with NumPy and SymPy installed. It reconstructs all spectral constraints and checks exact integer/rational inequalities. `fast_search.py` and `build_certificate.py` are optional discovery code requiring SciPy; `explore.py` provides the discovery data generator. The initial older search for q=385 timed out, and the first 12-second LP attempt for q=561 timed out; the later 35-second attempt found a feasible solution. No infeasibility inference is made. None of the four results has been checked in Lean or by an independent human reviewer. The finite results do not prove a classification for all triple-prime moduli, nor cover even triple-prime moduli. The general geometric lemma and noise correction apply conditional on the stronger phase law. The literature search checked Kang's original theorem and targeted public searches for the four cases and timing noise; no matching result was located, but priority is not established and the search is not exhaustive. This record is intentionally not presented as a completed paper or uploaded publicly.
