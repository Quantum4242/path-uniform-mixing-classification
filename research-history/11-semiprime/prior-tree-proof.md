# Research note: overlapping cyclotomic constraints and random spanning-tree completion

27 November 2026. Working draft, no author assigned. AI-assisted, not peer-reviewed, no full Lean formalization. Not a publication release. Prior work on prime-power odd parts is separate.

## 1. Problem and results
For q=N+1>=3 let A be the adjacency matrix of P_N. We ask whether a probability law on times produces E|exp(itA)|^{∘2}=J/N. Kang (arXiv:2609.14463v1) established existence for q prime, twice prime or a power of 2; nonexistence for 6|q with q>=12, or 15|q or 21|q. His forced-coherence lemma requires (and suffices for) mean cos((theta_r-theta_s)t)=-1/N for distinct same-parity r,s, and 0 otherwise. Here theta_r=2cos(r*pi/q).

This note establishes, subject to independent review, the following new results:

**Theorem A (exact finite certificates).** The positive cases q=33,35,39,55,65,70,77,85,91,95,110,130,140,154 all admit a finite probability law on nonnegative integer times with exact uniform average mixing. The JSON files and independent full-pair verifier are part of the computer-assisted proof. They certify a phase law, not explicit integer-time samples.

**Theorem B (infinite odd semiprime family).** If q=pr for distinct odd primes p<r, and either p>=13 or p=11 and r>=37, P_(q-1) admits a finite probability law on nonnegative integer times with exact uniform average mixing. A general sufficient condition for smaller primes is given in Section 4; failure of this condition is not nonexistence. The proof uses a random union of three edge-disjoint spanning trees and elementary rational inequalities. It is an analytic construction, not a fit to finite numerical examples. The displayed polynomial positivity certificate can be audited without a numerical solver.

In either result at most m^2+1 integer times suffice for q odd, where m=(q-1)/2. For q even m=q/2-1 and the bound is m(m+1)+1. No minimality, maximal time bound, or explicit times for these new cases are claimed. No general classification for all q is claimed.

## 2. General bridge from a phase law to actual integer times
Let theta be the vector of real eigenvalues, and H the closure in the mode phase torus of {exp(it theta):t real}. The annihilating integer characters of H are exactly k with k dot theta=0 (Kronecker's character theorem). Since theta is algebraic and pi is transcendental, k dot theta in 2*pi*Z implies k dot theta=0. Hence the closure of the integer-time orbit is H as well.

Write F for the vector of all distinct-mode cosine differences. Haar on H has mean F=0 because all theta_r-theta_s are nonzero. As H is compact, conv F(H) is compact, and 0 is in its relative interior: a supporting affine functional attaining its boundary value at the Haar mean must attain it everywhere by continuity and full support. If nu on H realizes same-parity cosine -b and opposite-parity cosine 0 with b>1/N, the law rho=nu/(Nb)+(1-1/(Nb))*Haar gives the target in that relative interior. The convex hull of the actual integer-time F values is dense in conv F(H), so the two relative interiors coincide. Thus the target belongs to the actual convex hull. Cosine symmetry replaces negative times by nonnegative times. Caratheodory gives at most one more than the number of distinct positive gap frequencies; the formal gap forms are theta_i +/- theta_j, 2theta_i and (even q only) theta_i on the positive spectrum, yielding the bounds stated above. This bridge is an extension to integer sampling of the standard torus/relative interior argument used by Kang.

## 3. Finite certificates via Galois averaging
For even q use the power-basis coefficient vectors V_r in Z[X]/Phi_(2q) of X^r+X^(2q-r). For odd q the positive-mode coordinates can be sign-adjusted and reordered to alpha_j=2cos(2*pi*j/q), 1<=j<=(q-1)/2, using Phi_q instead: theta_r=alpha_(r/2) for even r and -alpha_((q-r)/2) for odd r. Choose an integer seed vector a of length deg Phi with entries 0,1,2,3. Its phases are i^(V_r dot a). Such phases belong to H: every integer relation between the real eigenvalues is a coefficient-vector relation by cyclotomic irreducibility. Its dot product with a is zero, hence the corresponding product of phases is one. This is independent of whether the full integer coefficient map is surjective onto a finite code.

The Galois group acts on all modes by r -> min(u*r mod 2q, -u*r mod 2q), u a unit modulo 2q. This is a permutation preserving parity; permuting a valid seed phase by this action remains in H. Average each seed's probability uniformly over this finite group. Only one moment per pair orbit is needed. The search uses a floating-point LP for discovery, followed by exact rational linear-system solving and positivity checks. In every certificate the auxiliary target b is 1/(q-2)>1/N.

The independent verifier does not use the LP orbit partition or recurrence for polynomial coefficients. It uses polynomial remainder over Z and reconstructs every original spectral mode. For each seed and every unit, it evaluates all cosines exactly in {0,1,-1}, checks positive rational weights summing to 1, and checks every original mode pair directly with integer arithmetic. The NumPy products are over bounded small integers; all weighted sums use arbitrary-precision Python ints. Together with Section 2 and Kang's sufficient criterion, the finite certificates prove Theorem A if the checker and elementary derivations are correct. Negative controls q=12,15,21,30 reproduce exact collisions using the cyclotomic coefficients, not only a failed LP.

## 4. Odd semiprimes as a graph incidence code
Let q=pr, p<r distinct odd primes. Put P=(p-1)/2, R=(r-1)/2, m=(pr-1)/2=2PR+P+R. Use sign-adjusted coordinates alpha_j from Section 3. A coordinate corresponds to a nonzero residue modulo q, identifying j and -j. By CRT, this is a pair (a,b) in Z_p x Z_r modulo simultaneous negation. For each of P nonzero row classes ±a, summing over b in Z_r gives a relation among alpha coordinates with coefficients 1. Similarly for each of R nonzero column classes ±b. The root-of-unity geometric sums give zero. The coordinate (a,0) appears in just its row relation, and (0,b) just its column relation; these prove independence. The alpha_j span the real cyclotomic field: their sum is -1, and the sums of powers with their conjugates span the real subfield over Q (apply conjugation averaging to a spanning set of powers). Its dimension is (p-1)(r-1)/2=2PR. Hence the relation dimension is m-2PR=P+R, so the displayed relations span all rational relations. They also generate the integral relation lattice, since the axis coordinates extract the individual coefficients of any integral relation.

We can represent the relation matrix, after negating column rows, as a reduced incidence matrix for a multigraph with P row vertices, R column vertices and a root vertex o. Every (a,b) with both nonzero gives two parallel edges from a row to a column, and each axis coordinate gives one edge from/to the root. All orientations are chosen to reproduce the signed incidence coefficients. H in the alpha coordinates consists exactly of angle vectors whose sums satisfy this incidence constraint modulo 2*pi.

Work with phases z=i^x, x in Z/4Z. The target in the sign-adjusted coordinates is E Re(z_i conjugate(z_j))=-b for i!=j and E Re(z_i z_j)=0 for every i,j (including i=j). Indeed theta modes comprise one +alpha_j and one -alpha_j, with the positive sign at an even index and negative at an odd index. The target on the original spectral pairs follows, for any b.

### 4.1 Three disjoint reserve trees
Choose ordered triples of distinct rows a1,a2,a3 and distinct columns b1,b2,b3, uniformly and independently. Label the two parallel edges in each cell + and -, then apply an independent fair swap of the labels in each cell. Tree i consists of all R + edges at row ai, the P-1 - edges at column bi from rows other than ai, and one root edge incident to ai or bi (choose fairly and independently). It is connected, contains P+R edges, and is a spanning tree. The three trees are edge-disjoint: distinct row stars use distinct + edges, distinct column stars distinct - edges, and their root edges differ. Reserve their union, and call every other coordinate active. The number of active coordinates is fixed, M=m-3(P+R). The reserve graph stays connected if any two reserved edges are deleted.

For any active angle choices in Z/4Z, uniform completion on the reserves subject to the incidence constraint exists: a spanning tree solves arbitrary reduced incidence right-hand sides by leaf elimination, over Z/4Z (no division needed). Moreover, even after prescribing any two reserve angles arbitrarily, the remaining graph still contains an intact spanning tree. Thus the projection of the affine solution set to any two reserve coordinates is surjective, with equal-size fibers. The reserves are individually uniform and pairwise independent conditional on all active angles. This is a finite analogue of low-order marginal preservation in a parity-check code. This assertion does not require a field structure on Z/4Z.

### 4.2 Random matching on active coordinates
Add a dummy if M is odd; choose a uniform perfect matching on this augmented set. Write K=M-1 if M even and K=M if M odd; conditional on both active any given pair is matched with probability 1/K. Let beta_ij be the probability that two specified coordinates are both active. Give each matched pair independent of the others a distribution with uniform fourth-root marginals: with probability D_ij choose a uniform root and its negative, otherwise choose two independent uniform roots. Then E Re(z_i conjugate(z_j))=-D_ij, E Re(z_i z_j)=0 and each first and second complex moment vanishes. Discard dummy if present. A coordinate that is reserved has zero first and second moments and is pairwise uniform with other reserves and with active coordinates conditional on the active values. Consequently the unconditional moments are exactly -beta_ij D_ij/K for differences, zero for all sums. Set b=1/(q-2)=1/(2m-1) and D_ij=bK/beta_ij. This is feasible if every beta_ij >= K/(2m-1); we use the sufficient strict inequality beta_ij > M/(2m-1). The complete phase law is finite (the probabilities D_ij rational). Section 2 gives integer-time uniform average mixing.

### 4.3 Exact pair probabilities and sufficient ranges
For a cell (a,b), let A indicate row a is selected as a center, B column b, and C their being a paired center (same triple index). Its reserve multiplicity L=A+B-C. After the fair swap, a specified parallel edge has probability E(L)/2 of being reserved, with E=3/P+3/R-3/(PR). The active pair probability for two parallel edges of one cell is beta_parallel=(1-3/P)(1-3/R). For edges in distinct cells, independent label swaps conditional on centers give beta=1-E+E(L1*L2)/4. The three joint expressions, from counting ordered triples, are:

J_row=3/P+18/(PR)+6/[R(R-1)]-6/(PR)*(1+2/(R-1)),

J_col=J_row with P,R exchanged,

J_diff=6/[P(P-1)]+6/[R(R-1)]+18/(PR)-6/(PR)*(2/(P-1)+2/(R-1))+6/[P(P-1)R(R-1)].

For example, when both rows and columns differ, E(A1 A2)=6/[P(P-1)], E(A1 B2)=9/(PR), E(C1 A2)=3/(PR)*2/(P-1), E(C1 B2)=3/(PR)*2/(R-1), E(C1 C2)=6/[P(P-1)R(R-1)]; expansion of L1 L2 yields J_diff. If the rows coincide, E(A1 A2)=3/P and E(C1 C2)=0, yielding J_row. The probability of an axis edge being reserved is at most 3/(2P) since P<=R. Hence the union bound gives beta_axis,interior >=1-3/(2P)-E/2 and beta_axes >=1-3/P. These six expressions cover all distinct coordinate pairs. If each exceeds M/(2m-1), the construction proves existence. This is a general explicit sufficient condition for odd semiprimes with P,R>=3 and M>=2.

The accompanying `verify_tree.py` gives an exact symbolic certificate that all six inequalities hold throughout P>=6,R>=8, and P=5,R>=18. It subtracts M from (2m-1) times each expression, shifts variables by the boundary values, and verifies that the numerator and denominator polynomials have nonnegative coefficients and strictly positive constants. The printed polynomials make these finite algebraic checks inspectable. It independently enumerates small ordered center triples to check the tree property and the four exact interior pair rates. The symbolic inequalities are proofs over the full ranges, not finite sampling of large primes. Since distinct primes p>=13 and r>p imply P>=6,R>=8, and p=11,r>=37 gives P=5,R>=18, Theorem B follows. This bound on primes is sufficient, not sharp.

## 5. Status and limits
The previous prime-power note's four checkers were rerun successfully; this does not independently certify all its general probabilistic arguments. The current note gives a separate new construction for overlapping relations in odd semiprimes. No assertion of full classification or of Lean verification. A possible next step is to use biased internal matchings or more economical reserve systems for the small prime cases and to extend the graph-incidence construction to prime powers and three distinct odd factors. The finite certificates show that a range of odd and even overlapping cases is feasible, even with p=3 in odd q. These observations motivate the conjecture that parity collisions may be the only obstruction, but do not prove it. Recheck the original literature and all subsequent results before publication. Searches for semiprime, spanning-tree, and individual path uniform average mixing found no matching earlier result among returned sources; this is not a priority guarantee.
